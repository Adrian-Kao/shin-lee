# AI Transparency / Explainability UX — PatentMind

> Companion to `docs/PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` (§10 trust band, §11 visual identity) and `docs/UX_RESEARCH.md` (§4.2 citation grounding). This doc is about the **surface area** of trust: how the UI lets a US/TW patent attorney verify a draft fast enough to actually use it, without ever experiencing the model as a black box.
>
> Audience: design + frontend (Phase 4/5), partner counsel review board, eventual security review. Roughly 4,000 words.

---

## 0. Why this doc exists

`docs/PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` argues the trust band is the wedge. `docs/UX_RESEARCH.md` catalogs what competitors ship. `CLAUDE.md` §4 invariant 5 codifies the *backend* contract: "Citations in drafts MUST come from the grounded set" — `backend/ai_engine/oa_analyzer.py::verify_citations` is the hard wall.

This doc is the **UI side of that wall**. Patent attorneys (per [ABA Formal Opinion 512, July 2024](https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2024/07/aba-issues-first-ethics-guidance-ai-tools/) and the [USPTO April 2024 AI guidance](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/11/2024-07629/guidance-on-use-of-artificial-intelligence-based-tools-in-practice-before-the-united-states-patent)) are now under an explicit professional duty to verify every AI-assisted assertion before signing. The product wins or loses on whether the verification UI is **frictionless** — not by hiding uncertainty but by surfacing it so usefully that the attorney experiences the AI as a hyper-competent junior associate.

## 1. The trust-collapse failure mode

Four public events define the legal-industry baseline for "what AI must never let happen":

1. **Mata v. Avianca, 678 F. Supp. 3d 443 (S.D.N.Y. 2023)** — counsel filed a brief with six fabricated case citations from ChatGPT; Judge Castel sanctioned each attorney $5,000 under Rule 11 for "subjective bad faith." ([opinion PDF](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/archive/2025/12/Mata-v-Avianca-Inc.pdf), [Esquire summary](https://www.esquiresolutions.com/federal-court-turns-up-the-heat-on-attorneys-using-chatgpt-for-research/)) **UI failure moment:** ChatGPT presented prose with no source pin. Nothing in the interface said "this case has not been verified against any database."
2. **Park v. Kim, 91 F.4th 610 (2d Cir. 2024)** — Attorney Lee cited *Matter of Bourguignon*, a case that does not exist; the Second Circuit referred her to its Grievance Panel. ([Reason/Volokh](https://reason.com/volokh/2024/01/30/second-circuit-refers-lawyer-for-disciplinary-proceedings-based-on-ai-hallucinated-case-in-brief/), [FDLI summary](https://www.fdli.org/2025/08/park-v-kim/)) **UI failure moment:** no one-click "open the source" affordance. The verification path was copy-paste into Westlaw — a context switch nobody actually does.
3. **USPTO April 11, 2024 guidance** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/11/2024-07629/guidance-on-use-of-artificial-intelligence-based-tools-in-practice-before-the-united-states-patent); preceded by Director Vidal's February 2024 PTAB/TTAB memo) — every USPTO filing assertion, including AI-assisted ones, is subject to the duty of candor (37 C.F.R. §§ 11.18, 11.303); practitioners "must double-check any work product generated by AI." **UI failure moment:** today's workflow is "AI drafts → attorney skims → attorney signs." Without per-sentence affordances, "skim" replaces "verify."
4. **ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024)** ([opinion PDF](https://www.lawnext.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf)) — Rule 1.1 competence + Rule 5.1/5.3 supervision now apply to GenAI; lawyers must have "a reasonable understanding of the capabilities and limitations of the specific GAI technology." **UI failure moment:** most tools do not expose *which model* produced *which sentence*, so supervising partners cannot fulfill Rule 5.1 even when they want to.

**Five UI failure modes PatentMind must prevent (become principles §10):**

- **F1: Inline fact with no source pin.** Prose says "in TIPO reexamination 110年訴字第123號..." with no link, chip, or warning. Fix: every claim-bearing sentence carries a citation chip or a "no source — please verify" badge. No silent fallthrough.
- **F2: Source pin pointing to a synthesised passage.** Citation hover displays text that doesn't appear in the cited document. Fix: hover preview renders from the *retrieved chunk*, not the model output. Click-through scrolls right pane to the exact paragraph offset.
- **F3: Multiple sources collapsed into one citation.** Draft says "see prior art" linking only to US7654321 when the model used three references. Fix: multi-source cluster pattern (§2.7) — `[¹⁻³]` with stacked hover card. Over-confidence is structurally impossible.
- **F4: Confidence as a number with no next step.** "Confidence: 0.72." Useless. Fix: confidence expressed as a *verification ladder rung* (§6): "Read the cited passage" / "Run a second model" / "Open the audit row." Every confidence display links to an action.
- **F5: Hidden uncertainty.** Model produces a draft for a §103 rejection with only one weak retrieval hit; UI shows draft with no warning. Fix: uncertainty UX (§5) — explicit "no prior art with claim-overlap > 0.6 found" callouts. Drafts may be *skipped*, not faked.

## 2. Provenance patterns — the citation grammar

`docs/UX_RESEARCH.md` §4.2 surveyed the [Shape of AI citation taxonomy](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/citations) and the Granola/Patlytics/CoCounsel implementations. Below we evaluate ten patterns specifically against the PatentMind context (claim charts, multi-jurisdiction, signed drafts).

### 2.1 Inline footnote marker `[¹]` + hover card
Superscript number after the claim or sentence; hover opens a card with patent number, section, retrieval score, and the first ~200 chars of the retrieved passage. Dense; doesn't disrupt prose; works in print export. Hover-only is hostile to touch / keyboard, so the marker must be a `<button>` (not span) with `aria-describedby` pointing to the card; the card must be reachable via `Tab` and dismissable with `Esc`. **SHIP — primary pattern.** Precedents: [Anthropic Citations API](https://claude.com/blog/introducing-citations-api), [CoCounsel citation ledger](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/cocounsel-legal-reimagined/).

### 2.2 Underline + side-rail margin notes
Notion / Google Workspace pattern — gentle underline on grounded spans; right rail lists notes anchored to those underlines. Conflicts with our three-pane layout (`docs/UX_RESEARCH.md` §4.4). **REJECT.**

### 2.3 Sentence-level highlight tied to source pane
Granola / Patlytics — click a sentence in the draft; the corresponding source paragraph highlights in the right pane. Zero inline chrome, one-click verification, but invisible in printed PDF. ([Shape of AI citations](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/citations)) **SHIP as secondary pattern paired with 2.1.**

### 2.4 Selection-anchored sidebar
Linear AI — select a span, get a side-anchored card with "edit citation / remove / re-ground" actions. Powerful for editing but requires text-selection (not discoverable for first-time users). **SHIP in v2.**

### 2.5 "Snap to source"
Cursor / Claude Projects style — click a citation chip → right pane scrolls to that paragraph, highlights it, places a cursor anchor. The highest-value attorney action (verify the source) becomes one click. Source pane must persist; works poorly on small screens. **SHIP — non-negotiable. This is L1 of the verification ladder (§6).**

### 2.6 Source confidence pip (1–3 dots)
Each chip carries `● ● ●` (high retrieval similarity), `● ● ○` (medium), `● ○ ○` (low). More meaningful than a number; calibrates trust *before* the click. Still ordinal, but ordinal is honest. **SHIP — pip on every citation chip; the retrieval-confidence signal of §3.**

### 2.7 Multi-source cluster
`[¹⁻³]` rendered as a stacked chip; expand to see all three sources. Prevents F3 (collapsed citations). `DraftResponse.grounded_citations` in `backend/shared/models.py` is already a list. **SHIP.**

### 2.8 Quote-vs-paraphrase distinction
Block-quote glyph `❝` for verbatim text; underline-only for paraphrase. Verbatim text in monospace (matches our typography stack — `docs/PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` §5.2). Lets attorney distinguish "cheap to verify" from "must read the source." Needs `RetrievalHit.metadata` tag we don't emit today. **SHIP v2 — flag for backend work.**

### 2.9 Attorney-edit lock
When the attorney edits a cited sentence, the citation pin removes itself and a warning chip replaces it: "Citation removed — sentence diverged from model output. [Re-ground] / [Discard citation]." Structurally prevents the Mata v. Avianca failure (lawyer hand-edits but keeps the AI citation). Need a diff threshold to avoid triggering on trivial edits. **SHIP — DraftEditor invariant (§8).**

### 2.10 Failed-citation marker
Red strikethrough on the citation chip with "remove or re-ground" action. Backend already emits `[CITATION_REMOVED]` (see `oa_analyzer.py::verify_citations` and `DraftEditor.jsx::CitationHighlighter`); we just need to make it actionable rather than passive. Verifier disagreement is *never silent* (principle #3). **SHIP — already half-built.**

**The three canonical patterns we adopt:** (2.1) inline footnote + hover card, (2.5) snap-to-source, (2.6) confidence pip. (2.3, 2.7, 2.9, 2.10) come along for free because the data model supports them.

## 3. Confidence visualisation — beyond the single 0.72

`backend/shared/models.py::DraftResponse` currently carries one float `confidence`. Lawyers don't trust scores ([Bansal et al. 2021 CHI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779): explanations increase acceptance regardless of correctness; a bare confidence number primes the same trap). We need a richer language.

| Signal | Source | UI affordance | Token |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retrieval confidence** | `RetrievalHit.score` (cosine vs query embedding) | 3-pip glyph on each citation chip (§2.6). | `● ● ●` `● ● ○` `● ○ ○` `○ ○ ○` |
| **Reasoning confidence** | `DraftResponse.confidence` returned by the drafting LLM | Expandable disclosure "Why I argued this" beneath the draft (§4). | Disclosure chevron `▸` |
| **Verifier agreement** | `verify_citations` returns `valid`, `verifier_confidence` (`oa_analyzer.py` line ~208) | Coloured ribbon under the draft: green = passed, amber = passed-with-warnings, red = verifier rejected. | `--color-success` / `--color-accent` / `--color-danger` |
| **Citation grounding** | regex extraction + `_STATUTE_WHITELIST` check (`oa_analyzer.py` lines 142-146) | Inline `✓` or `✗` immediately after each citation chip. | Lucide `check-circle-2` / `x-circle` |

Mapping rules:

- A pip `● ● ●` means similarity ≥ 0.80; `● ● ○` means 0.60-0.80; `● ○ ○` means 0.40-0.60; `○ ○ ○` means < 0.40 (and we should not have shipped this citation — see F5).
- Verifier ribbon copy is in plain language: "Verifier (Claude Haiku) agrees" / "Verifier flagged 2 citations" / "Verifier rejected this draft — please regenerate."
- Reasoning confidence does NOT show a number. We expose only "the model self-rated this as a strong/medium/uncertain argument" — three bands. (Calibration of internal model self-ratings is unreliable; binary self-ratings are honest about that uncertainty.)

**Comparison with competitors:** ChatGPT surfaces no confidence (fail). [Claude.ai Citations](https://claude.com/blog/introducing-citations-api) surfaces source pins beautifully but no confidence layering (partial). Cursor shows model attribution per turn but no confidence per assertion (partial). [Linear's Triage Intelligence](https://linear.app/now) uses ambient colour cues instead of numbers (strong — borrow the philosophy). Notion AI shows nothing (fail).

The multi-signal approach is the principled response to [Buçinca et al.'s finding](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3449287) that partial explanations + cognitive forcing reduce overreliance better than full explanations. Each signal forces a small cognitive step ("what does this pip mean?") that triggers verification thinking before acceptance.

## 4. The "show me your work" disclosure

A collapsible reasoning trail per draft. Spec:

- **Default state: collapsed.** Partners do not want clutter. Disclosure is opt-in. ([Buçinca 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692) and the partial-explanations follow-up indicate that *forcing* full explanations on every output backfires.)
- **Trigger:** chevron `▸ Show reasoning` at the bottom of each draft.
- **Contents when expanded:**
  1. **Which OA rejection rule fired** — e.g., `RejectionType.OBVIOUSNESS_103` with `affected_claims=[1, 7]` and `examiner_argument` quote.
  2. **Retrieval trace** — top 5 hits with rank, score, patent_no, section, *and a `[skipped because…]` line* for any hit that was retrieved but not used in the draft (this is the under-shipped UX — most tools hide the rejection set; we show it).
  3. **Selected canonical citation** — the hit that ended up as `[GROUNDED_REF_1]`, with a one-line reason ("highest claim-overlap" / "only one with date < priority date" / "TIPO-Chinese passage matched").
  4. **Verifier output** — the JSON from `verify_citations` (`valid`, `valid_citations`, `invalid_citations`, `verifier_confidence`).
  5. **Model attribution per step** — `parse_oa: claude-haiku-4-7`, `draft_response: claude-opus-4-7[1m]`, `verify_citations: claude-haiku-4-7`. This satisfies ABA 512's Rule 5.1 supervisory expectation that partners be able to determine *which model produced what*.
  6. **Pattern reuse callout** — "This argument follows the *unexpected results* framing we have used in 4 successful TIPO reexamination decisions: 110-訴-456, 111-訴-201, ...". Concrete, attorney-trustworthy framing. Comes from our seed `data/decisions/` corpus (we have 7 reexam decisions per `feature/patentmind-poc` recent commit `a512831`).

**Comparison:**
- **ChatGPT** "Show reasoning" disclosure (o1/o3 family) is plain text; lacks structured retrieval traces. We do better because we *have* retrieval traces.
- **Claude `<thinking>` reveal** is excellent for chain-of-thought, but doesn't pin to a retrieval set.
- **Cursor tool-call panel** is structurally similar but model-call-centric, not source-centric.
- **Granola transcript reveal** is the closest analogue — "hovering over a summary and clicking the magnifying glass opens a pop-up with the full transcript context" ([Bluedot Granola review](https://www.bluedothq.com/blog/granola-review)).

**Mock-up (expanded panel):**
```
▸ Show reasoning  (3 retrievals, 1 verifier check, ~$0.04)
  Rule fired:  §103 obviousness on claims [1, 7]
  Retrieved:   1. case spec § 0024     0.87  ● ● ●  selected
               2. case claim 1         0.81  ● ● ●  selected
               3. US7654321 col 4      0.83  ● ● ●  examined
               4. US7654321 col 6      0.61  ● ● ○  skipped:
                  "off-topic — describes thermal control"
               5. EP1234567 ¶ 0041     0.45  ● ○ ○  skipped:
                  "filed after priority date 2018-03-22"
  Verifier:    claude-haiku-4-7 ✓ all 2 citations grounded
  Pattern:     "unexpected results" — succeeded in TIPO
               110-訴-456, 111-訴-201, 112-訴-088, 113-訴-115
  Models:      parse=haiku  draft=opus  verify=haiku
```

## 5. Uncertainty UX — the most under-shipped category

When the AI doesn't know, the UI must say so in a way that doesn't undermine confidence in the tool overall. The Mata v. Avianca failure was structurally an uncertainty-UX failure: ChatGPT had no signal for "I am making this up" because confabulation is silent.

### 5.1 The skipped section
When the retrieval set has no hit with claim-overlap > 0.6 for a given rejection, **do not draft a response.** Render instead:

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Rejection R-4 (§102, claim 14) — NOT DRAFTED
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  We retrieved 3 candidates but none overlap your claim
  text by > 60%. Drafting a response here would require us
  to argue from unfamiliar prior art.
  
  [Open retrieval set]  [Force-draft anyway]  [Skip rejection]
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

The `Force-draft anyway` button is intentionally present but the language ("Force") is honest about what the attorney is doing.

### 5.2 Multiple-draft variants
For rejections with three or more strong but divergent retrieval clusters, offer three drafts side-by-side instead of one confident-looking answer:

- **Variant A (narrowing):** argue the claim should be amended to add `<missing limitation>`.
- **Variant B (distinguishing):** argue the prior art teaches away.
- **Variant C (procedural):** challenge the examiner's combination as lacking motivation.

Attorney picks one; the audit row records which variant was chosen and which were considered.

### 5.3 Unfamiliar examiner pattern
When the OA's argument style appears in fewer than N similar cases in our reexamination corpus, surface:

```
⚠ Unfamiliar examiner pattern
The examiner's combination logic ("teaches in tandem with") appears
in only 2 of our 1,200 indexed TIPO reexam decisions. Templates
below may not match well; partner review strongly recommended.
```

### 5.4 Confidence-degraded mode
When `llm_client.py` returns `circuit_open=True` (we are degraded — Anthropic API rate-limit, on-prem inference saturated), the UI hides some features and explains why:

```
[Banner, amber]
🛡 Degraded mode: verifier model unavailable.
  • Drafts will run but the verifier ribbon will say "unverified".
  • Pattern-reuse callouts disabled.
  • Audit row still records normally.
[Retry in 30s]   [Continue with caveat]
```

`docs/PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` §7.3 already calls for surfacing the security routing band; uncertainty banners live in the same band region — see CHUNK-1/8 there.

## 6. The verification ladder

For every AI output, the attorney must be able to verify at progressive depth. Design this as a five-rung ladder where every rung is **one click away** from the original output.

| Rung | What | UI affordance | Estimated time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0** | Glance — is the answer plausible | Read the draft + ribbons + pips | 10–30 s |
| **L1** | Read the cited passage | Click footnote chip → right pane scrolls and highlights the source paragraph (§2.5 snap-to-source) | 30–90 s |
| **L2** | Read the full source patent | "Open in viewer" in the source pane → PDF / patent-DB modal | 5–20 min |
| **L3** | Run a second model and diff | "Re-run with [model picker]" in the reasoning disclosure (§4); diff view aligns sentence-by-sentence | 1–3 min |
| **L4** | Open the audit chain row | "View audit entry" link in the reasoning disclosure footer; deep-links to `AuditView.jsx` with the row pre-filtered | 30 s |

[Cursor's "compare across models" pattern](https://frontendmasters.com/blog/choosing-the-right-model-in-cursor/) and Phoenix Arize's trace UX are the precedents for L3 and L4 respectively. The ladder design is the structural answer to "the attorney should be able to spend exactly as much verification effort as the stakes demand" — for a routine §112 antecedent basis rejection, L0 + a quick L1 is enough; for a §101 patentable-subject-matter argument, the partner walks all the way to L4 before signing.

This ladder maps directly to the `AuditEntry` schema in `backend/shared/models.py`: `request_hash`, `response_hash`, `model_used`, and `policy_decisions` give us everything L4 needs.

## 7. AI-as-junior-associate framing

A consistent metaphor across the product: PatentMind is your most capable junior associate. They draft fast, cite well, and need partner review before anything leaves the firm.

| Surface | Today's copy | Proposed copy |
|---|---|---|
| Draft arrives | "Analysis complete." | "Draft ready for partner review." |
| Empty state | "No drafts yet." | "Associate is drafting…" / "Associate hasn't started — upload an OA to begin." |
| Error state | "Error 500: backend unavailable." | "Associate couldn't finish — the back-office is unreachable. We'll keep your input." |
| Audit log title | "Audit Trail" | "Associate's daily memo" *(also* "Audit Trail" *visible as the technical/SOC-2 label)* |
| Sign button | "Sign" | "Sign off — accept responsibility for this filing." |

The sign-off interaction is **load-bearing**. Today `DraftEditor.jsx::sign` just sets `signed=true`. The proposed flow:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Sign off on Draft R-2                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ By signing, you attest:                                  │
│  ☐ I have read the cited passages for all 3 citations.   │
│  ☐ I have reviewed the verifier ribbon (green ✓).        │
│  ☐ I am ready to file this with USPTO / TIPO on my       │
│    professional credentials.                             │
│                                                          │
│ This draft contains:                                     │
│  • 12 AI-generated sentences                             │
│  • 4 attorney-rewritten sentences                        │
│  • 3 grounded citations                                  │
│  • Verifier passed at 0.94 confidence                    │
│                                                          │
│ Bar number (auto-filled): TW-12345  ✎                    │
│                                                          │
│ [Cancel]                          [Sign and lock]        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The three checkboxes are not legally binding (no AI tool checkbox replaces the duty of candor under USPTO 37 C.F.R. § 11.18) but they create a Ulysses contract: the attorney is reminded, in writing, what she is signing.

**Should the metaphor ship product-wide or stay at onboarding?**

The HCI literature on anthropomorphism is mixed. [Anthropomorphizing technical systems has been criticized for setting wrong expectations and causing over-reliance](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16047) — calling the AI "your associate" could prime exactly the wrong behaviour. Counter-evidence: domain-appropriate metaphors that are *honest about hierarchy* (junior, not peer) help users calibrate ([Lee & Sukoff CHI 2024 family of work on critical evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13970v1)).

**Recommendation: ship the metaphor at onboarding and on the sign-off ceremony only.** Body copy in the main analyse flow should be neutral ("Draft 3 of 4"), not "Your associate finished draft 3." The metaphor lives at the boundaries where partner agency matters most: starting work, signing work, reviewing the audit log.

## 8. Attorney-edit invariants

Today's `DraftEditor.jsx` already supports per-line `source: 'ai' | 'attorney'` provenance and a sign-off gate (every line must be accepted or rewritten). We extend it with four invariants:

1. **Citations move with the sentence they ground.** When the attorney drags a sentence or its position changes, the citation chip moves with the sentence text. If the sentence is split into two, the chip stays with whichever new sentence retains the cited assertion (heuristic: the chip stays with the half that contains the noun phrase the chip pinned).
2. **Confidence chip downgrades on edit.** Per `DraftEditor.jsx::commitEdit`, when `source` flips to `attorney`, the line's "verifier passed" ribbon becomes "verifier rerunning…" until the background re-check returns. The corresponding `AuditEntry` is written before the new verifier call.
3. **Audit row branches.** The original AI output is preserved in the `request_hash`/`response_hash` chain. The edited version writes a new `AuditEntry` with `endpoint=/draft/edit`, the attorney's `user_id`, and a hash pointing back to the original. The chain is append-only (`backend/gateway/audit.py`) so this is automatic — we just need to wire the new endpoint.
4. **Verifier re-runs on save.** A debounced background call to `verify_citations` runs on every commit; warning ribbon appears if the verifier flags the edited version. This is the structural answer to F2: even if the attorney introduces an unverifiable assertion in editing, the system catches it.

Pseudocode for the invariant guard on `commitEdit`:

```js
function commitEdit() {
  const original = lines[editingIdx];
  const edited = { ...original, source: 'attorney', text: editValue };
  edited.citations = filterCitationsByPresence(original.citations, editValue); // §8.1
  edited.verifier_state = 'rerunning';                                          // §8.2
  api.post('/audit/branch', { original_hash: original.hash,                     // §8.3
    edited_hash: hashSentence(editValue), user_id: currentUser.user_id });
  setLines((ls) => ls.map((l, idx) => idx === editingIdx ? edited : l));
  scheduleReverify(edited);                                                     // §8.4
}
```

## 9. Comparative teardowns

We walk through six competitor screens and annotate by region. Layouts described from public product pages, reviews, and `docs/UX_RESEARCH.md` §2.

### 9.1 Patlytics — claim chart with inline citations
Three-pane: claim list left, claim-↔-reference middle, source PDF right. Per-claim "cited by N refs" badge in the left rail; hover tooltip on the middle pane shows the citation passage.
- **Copy:** the three-pane layout and per-claim citation count.
- **Reject:** floating tooltip on hover (overlaps middle pane at < 1280px) and numeric scores (`0.83`) that imply false precision.
- **Leapfrog:** replace numeric scores with pip glyph (§2.6); use right-pane scroll instead of hover for the source preview.

### 9.2 ClaimMaster — claim tree with rejection colour overlay
Vertical tree: independent claims as roots, dependents as branches, each coloured by rejection state (red/amber/green).
- **Copy:** the tree itself + colour mapping. `ClaimNode` / `ClaimTree.jsx` already match this; `docs/UX_RESEARCH.md` §4.1 calls it out.
- **Reject:** ClaimMaster's three-views approach (bubble, dynamic, text) confuses partners — pick one (text tree).
- **Leapfrog:** add per-node citation counts so the tree doubles as a citation index.

### 9.3 Solve Intelligence — single confidence number
Right-rail "Confidence: 0.78" beside each draft.
- **Copy:** nothing — this is the failure pattern (F4).
- **Reject:** bare number with no actionable next step.
- **Leapfrog:** four-signal layering (§3). The attorney sees retrieval pips, verifier ribbon, citation ticks, and reasoning disclosure — never a bare number.

### 9.4 Granola — hover citation peek
Hover on a summary line shows a popup with the source transcript timestamp and verbatim quote ([Bluedot review](https://www.bluedothq.com/blog/granola-review)).
- **Copy:** the hover → peek pattern is the gold standard for L0/L1.
- **Reject:** hover-only with no click action; doesn't satisfy our L2 rung (open the full source).
- **Leapfrog:** keep hover for L0/L1, add click → right-pane scroll for L1–L2.

### 9.5 Linear AI — ambient confidence
Coloured ambient pill (no number) on each suggestion; "Why?" disclosure on demand. Matches [Linear's design philosophy](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design/) — "boring and bettering UI."
- **Copy:** disclosure-on-demand for the reasoning trail (§4).
- **Reject:** ambient-only is too quiet for legal stakes.
- **Leapfrog:** combine ambient pill (retrieval) with explicit ribbon (verifier).

### 9.6 Cursor — verifier as model picker
Explicit model attribution in the composer at prompt time; per-turn model name in the chat history.
- **Copy:** explicit model attribution (ABA 512 supervisory duty requires this).
- **Reject:** model shown only at *prompt* time, not at *output* time.
- **Leapfrog:** show *multiple* model attributions per draft — parse → Haiku, draft → Opus, verify → Haiku per `route_model` in `llm_client.py`.

## 10. The seven transparency principles

1. **No floating claims without a source pin.** Every assertion in a draft body either carries a citation chip or is marked `[needs grounding]`. Silence is not allowed. (F1)
2. **No single-number confidence scores.** Confidence is always multi-signal: retrieval pip + verifier ribbon + citation tick + (optional) reasoning band. (F4, §3)
3. **Verifier disagreement is never silent.** A red verifier ribbon blocks sign-off until the attorney either re-grounds the citation, removes it, or explicitly overrides with a logged reason. (F2, §2.10)
4. **Show retrieval rank, not just retrieval score.** The reasoning disclosure (§4) shows rank 1-N with `selected` / `skipped because …` labels. The attorney sees what we *didn't* use, not just what we did. (§4 step 2)
5. **Citation pins are first-class navigable objects.** Tab order, keyboard focus, `Esc` dismissal, and the snap-to-source action are all keyboard-reachable. The L1 rung of the verification ladder is one keystroke. (§2.5, §6)
6. **Attorney edit immediately downgrades model attribution.** When the attorney edits a sentence, the verifier ribbon flips to "rerunning," the line's `source` flips to `attorney`, and the audit row branches. The original AI text is preserved. (§8, F2)
7. **Audit row is reachable from every AI output in ≤ 2 clicks.** Reasoning disclosure → "View audit entry" → `AuditView.jsx`. The L4 rung of the verification ladder is always two clicks away. (§6, `AuditView.jsx`)

These are the invariants. Anything that violates them is a bug, not a design alternative.

## Report (≤ 400 words)

**Three patterns to ship in the next two sprints:**

1. **Footnote chip + snap-to-source + retrieval pip** (§2.1 + §2.5 + §2.6 combined). This is the single highest-value bundle. The backend already returns `grounded_citations` and `RetrievalHit.score`; the frontend has the half-built `CitationHighlighter` in `DraftEditor.jsx`. Sprint cost: small. Impact: closes F1, F4, and (when paired with §2.10's failed-citation marker, which is already half-built) F2.
2. **Verifier ribbon under each draft** (§3 + §2.10). The backend already returns `valid`, `verifier_confidence`, `invalid_citations` from `verify_citations`. We just need to render a green/amber/red band and a one-line message. This makes principle #3 ("verifier disagreement is never silent") shippable in one sprint.
3. **Reasoning disclosure with retrieval trace** (§4). Default collapsed. Surfaces rank-1..N hits with `selected/skipped` labels and model attribution per step. This is the "show me your work" pattern that satisfies ABA 512 supervisory duty and visibly differentiates from Solve / Patlytics / DeepIP — none of whom show *what they didn't use*. Backend needs one new field on the response payload (the `skipped` reasons); frontend is a collapsible panel.

**The controversial one — ship it anyway:** the **sign-off ceremony** with the three checkboxes (§7). Partners will complain it slows them down. That is the *point*: it is a deliberate cognitive forcing function ([Buçinca et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692)) that converts the "skim and sign" pattern (Mata v. Avianca) into "read at least the cited passages and then sign." It also creates a defensible record of attorney attestation — useful in any subsequent malpractice or sanctions matter. Ship it. Make it skippable on second filings of the same case to reduce friction without removing the gate.

**The competitor pattern we should NOT copy: the single confidence number** (Solve Intelligence §9.3 / many LLM apps). It is professionally dangerous in our setting. A number primes acceptance ([Bansal et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779) — explanations including confidence raised acceptance regardless of correctness) and offers no actionable next step (F4). Replace it everywhere with the four-signal layering of §3. If anyone on the team proposes a single confidence display in a design review, point them at this section.
